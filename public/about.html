    About us

    Practical, source-checked guides for the people who run enterprise technology

    TechStack Lab is an independent publication based in the United Arab Emirates. We write for engineers, architects and IT leaders who have to make cloud, security and SaaS decisions — and live with them afterwards.

    Sourced to primary documents

    Every guide links to the vendor documentation, standards (NIST, IETF, CNCF) and official pricing pages it relies on, so you can check our work.

    Maintained, with a changelog

    Guides carry a version number and a dated record of every substantive change. Revisions are also published in our RSS feed.

    Independent of advertisers

    Advertisers never see, review or influence a guide before publication. Any sponsored content would be clearly labelled.

    What we publish

    A lot of enterprise technology writing is either vendor marketing or summaries of other summaries. We aim for the opposite: guides that explain how something actually works, where the costs and risks hide, and what to decide first, with the primary sources linked so you can verify every claim.

    We cover five areas: Cloud architecture, Cybersecurity, SaaS tools, DevOps and AI workflows.

    Who we are

    TechStack Lab is published by a small independent editorial team. Guides are credited to the TechStack Lab editorial team rather than to individual authors, and the team as a whole is accountable for what we publish. You can reach us at gbromano.5bdf@gmail.com or through our contact page.

    Editorial guidelines

    How we choose topics

    We prioritise questions readers face in production or in procurement: migrations, architecture decisions, cost, security trade-offs and tool selection. We don't republish press releases, and we only cover product launches when we can add analysis.

    How we research

    • Technical claims are checked against the vendor's own documentation or the relevant standard, and those sources are listed at the end of every guide.
    • Where a figure changes often — prices, quotas, regional availability — we say so and link to the official page rather than relying on a number that will go stale.
    • Code and configuration examples are written to match the documented syntax. Treat them as starting points: test them in a non-production environment before you rely on them.
    • Comparisons use the same criteria for every product, and we publish those criteria in the guide.

    How we use AI tools

    We use AI tools to help research and draft guides. Every draft is then checked against the primary sources it cites, edited, and approved by the editorial team before it is published. We correct errors openly when we find them — including errors in AI-assisted drafts — and record the fix in the guide's changelog.

    Updates

    Each guide shows its version number and the date its sources were last checked. A substantive change bumps the version and adds a dated entry to "What changed in this guide". Typos and formatting fixes are made without a changelog entry.

    Corrections policy

    If we get something wrong, we fix it promptly and say what changed. Substantive corrections are recorded in the guide's changelog with the date. To report an error, use our contact form or email gbromano.5bdf@gmail.com with the guide's link and the passage in question.

    Advertising and independence

    TechStack Lab is funded by display advertising served by Google AdSense. Ads are labelled "Advertisement" and are chosen by Google's systems, not by our editors. Advertisers have no input into what we cover or what we conclude. If we ever publish sponsored content or use affiliate links, they will be clearly labelled where they appear.
